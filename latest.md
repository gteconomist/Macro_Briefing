## Top of mind

Election Day is the dominant non-data event this week, with markets likely to stay in a holding pattern until results clarify the fiscal and trade policy outlook. On the macro side, oil is the most pressing variable: WTI front-month settled at $104.12 Friday (+$2.12 on the day), Brent at $117.64 (+$2.76), levels that put renewed upward pressure on headline inflation just as the Fed is calibrating its terminal posture. The 10-year Treasury closed Friday at 4.94%, with the 10y/3m spread at +78 bps — the curve is now unambiguously positive after a prolonged inversion — and Friday's gold settlement of ~$3,876 (off $4.36 on the day) alongside silver near $49.13 (off $9.14) suggests some late-week commodity risk-off. The week's macro anchor is Friday's Employment Report, but the ISM Manufacturing print this morning at 10:00 will set the tone for the open.

- **Thu 11/5:** Productivity and Costs (8:30), Initial Jobless Claims (8:30) — Q3 productivity and unit labor costs are critical context for the ECI released Friday; claims (194K as of 10/24) have been remarkably low and any uptick warrants attention.

September payrolls came in at 159,044K (+29K), unemployment at 4.2%, and average hourly earnings at $32.60. October's print will land against the backdrop of election results and WTI above $100, making the wages and participation numbers (61.8% in September) especially consequential.

## Fed watch

The target range sits at 3.75–4.00% (DFEDTARU/DFEDTARL as of 11/1), with the effective fed funds rate at 3.88% (as of 10/29) and SOFR at 3.94% — both consistent with a range comfortably centered. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the week-ahead calendar, so the Fed is in a quiet observation posture heading into the election and Friday's jobs data. With WTI above $100, core PCE month-over-month re-accelerating (130.902 vs. 130.489 in August), and the 10-year at 4.94%, the market will be watching whether the October employment report gives the Committee any cover to stay on hold through year-end or forces a reassessment of the cutting cycle's pace.
