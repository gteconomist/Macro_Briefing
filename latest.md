## Top of mind

The dominant market story Thursday was a sharp bull-flattening of the Treasury curve: the 10-year dropped 17 bps to 4.96% and the 2-year fell 16 bps to 4.65%, while the 30-year shed 13 bps to 5.29% — moves large enough to demand an explanation in either a major risk-off catalyst or a significant data surprise, neither of which shows up clearly in the headlines payload. That said, T10Y3M spread compressed from 88 bps to 74 bps, still firmly positive, so no inversion signal. Simultaneously, WTI front-month settled at $99.47 (+$2.32) and Brent at $113.17 (+$4.35), pushing energy price pressure back into the conversation just as the rates rally might otherwise ease financial conditions. The combination of a rates rally and an oil spike is a stagflation-adjacent mix that warrants close attention heading into next week's data-heavy slate.

## Yesterday's releases (Thu 10/22)

- **Initial Jobless Claims (week ending 10/17):** 194,000 — down 6,000 from the prior week's 200,000; claims are tracking well below levels consistent with labor market deterioration, reinforcing the September payroll picture (PAYEMS +29K, UNRATE 4.2%) as indicative of slow but intact job growth rather than an emerging layoff cycle. Continuing claims (week ending 10/10) at 1.728M were up 25K from the prior week, a mild note of caution on re-employment speed.

## Today's calendar (Fri 10/23, ET)

- **Consumer Sentiment — Final (October), 10:00 ET** — The preliminary UMich read for August (the most recent FRED observation) sat at 51.7, already depressed; watch whether the final October print confirms or revises the preliminary and, critically, track the 1-year inflation expectations component — the preliminary MICH series last showed 4.0%, and any upward revision in a $99+ WTI environment would complicate the Fed's communications calculus.

## This week ahead

- **Mon 10/26:** Chicago Fed NAI (September) — useful cross-check on CFNAI, which was –0.04 for August, skirting the threshold for below-trend growth.
- **Tue 10/27:** Durable Goods Advance (September), Case-Shiller (August home prices), Consumer Confidence (October, Conference Board), New Home Sales (September) — the busiest day of the week; durable goods orders and consumer confidence are the highest-signal reads given the uncertainty around capex and household demand at current rates; new home sales will add texture to housing starts, which fell to 1,221K in September from 1,288K in August.

## Fed watch

Target range remains 3.75–4.00%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.88% and SOFR also at 3.88% (both as of 10/21) — no daylight between EFFR and SOFR, and both sitting 12 bps below the ceiling, consistent with ample reserves. Thursday's curve rally — 10-year at 4.96%, nearly 100 bps above the top of the target range — keeps real rate pressure elevated even after the session's move. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through the end of next week, so the Fed is effectively in a data-observation mode; next week's durable goods and confidence numbers will matter more for pricing the next move than anything on the communications calendar.
