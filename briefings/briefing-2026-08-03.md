## Top of mind

The dominant macro signal heading into August is the divergence between a still-robust real economy and mounting evidence that disinflation has taken hold. The GDPNow tracker sits at 4.95% for Q3 (vs. 1.54% prior quarter), headline CPI moved to 332.57 from 333.98 month-over-month (June), and core PCE is barely above flat at 130.27 vs. 130.09. Against that backdrop, oil is notable: WTI dropped to $84.25 from $91.74 and Brent to $91.82 from $100.31 in the span of a week — a disinflationary supply-side tailwind that will run through near-term headline readings. Senate appropriators reportedly reached a funding deal to avoid a shutdown, removing one tail risk from the near-term fiscal calendar.

## Fed watch

The target range holds at 3.50–3.75%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.63% and SOFR at 3.65% — both steady and well-anchored within the band. The 10Y/3M spread has widened to +92 bps (from +86 bps the prior day), continuing the bear steepening trend; the 10-year is at 4.68% and the 30-year at 5.21%, both up 1 bp on the week. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through this week, so the Fed is in a data-watching posture ahead of the Employment Report on Friday. The combination of sub-target core PCE, collapsing oil, and a Q3 GDPNow near 5% gives the Committee room to stay patient — but Friday's payroll and wage numbers will be the next real test of whether that patience gets challenged.
