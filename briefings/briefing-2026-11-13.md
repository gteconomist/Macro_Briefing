## Top of mind

The dominant story is the sustained backup in long-end yields. The 10-year Treasury closed Thursday at 5.10% — up 12 bps from Monday's close of 4.98% — and the 30-year hit 5.43%, also +8 bps on the week. The 2-year jumped 13 bps to 4.86%, meaning the curve is steepening from an already elevated level, with the 10y–3m spread now +78 bps vs. +68 bps prior. WTI front-month settled at $88.74 (+$2.21 on the day) and Brent at $95.82 (+$3.06), adding to the inflation anxiety already embedded in the rates move. This combination — term premium repricing plus an oil spike — is exactly the environment where the Fed's "higher for longer" posture gets stress-tested against incoming CPI/PPI prints.

## Fed watch

The target range remains 3.75–4.00%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.88% (as of 11/11) and SOFR at 3.89% (as of 11/10) — both running just below the ceiling, consistent with normal corridor mechanics. The 10-year at 5.10% is now 110 bps above the top of the target range, meaning financial conditions are tightening meaningfully beyond the policy rate itself. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through 11/17; the next scheduled catalyst in the payload is the data-heavy Tuesday. With oil ripping and PPI dropping this morning, any upside surprise in producer prices will intensify the debate over whether the current 4% ceiling is high enough — or whether the market is simply doing the Fed's work for it through the term premium channel.
