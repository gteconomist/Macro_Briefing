## Top of mind

Friday's Employment Report is the dominant print entering this week. August nonfarm payrolls came in at 159,075K — a +162K monthly gain — with unemployment holding at 4.1% and the participation rate ticking up 20 bps to 61.6%. Average hourly earnings rose $0.11 to $32.53, a ~0.34% MoM pace that keeps the wage-inflation picture manageable. The labor market read is solid enough to remove near-term recession urgency, but the more pressing overnight story is Iran's announced expansion of Strait of Hormuz control, which is driving energy prices sharply higher — WTI already at $91.48 (+$4.45 from prior close) and Brent at $96.02 (+$6.27) — a supply shock that complicates the Fed's path if it sustains.

## Fed watch

The target range sits at 3.50–3.75%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.63% and SOFR at 3.66% — both tracking cleanly within the corridor. The August jobs print gives the Committee no immediate pressure to move; a 4.1% unemployment rate and ~0.34% MoM wage growth are consistent with a patient hold. The more significant near-term risk is the Hormuz oil spike — if Brent holds above $95 into September CPI (Friday), headline inflation reversal will dominate the September FOMC discussion, and the rate-cut pricing that has built since early summer will face a re-test. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through the end of this week.
