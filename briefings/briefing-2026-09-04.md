## Top of mind

The August Employment Report drops at 8:30 this morning — the dominant data event of the week. The most recent FRED read (July) showed nonfarm payrolls essentially flat month-over-month (158,858K vs. 158,881K prior), unemployment ticking down to 4.1%, and average hourly earnings up a modest $0.04 to $32.40. Against that backdrop of softening labor demand, oil has surged sharply — WTI at $91.48 (+$4.45 from the prior observation), Brent at $96.02 (+$6.27) — injecting a stagflationary undercurrent into what the market needs to read as a clean labor print.

## Today's calendar (Fri 9/4, ET)

- **Employment Report, 8:30** — August NFP headline, unemployment rate, average hourly earnings m/m and y/y, and labor force participation. July's payrolls were essentially dead flat and participation slipped to 61.4%; a second consecutive weak print would sharpen the debate over whether the Fed is behind on easing. AHE acceleration would complicate that narrative given oil's move. The internals — full-time vs. part-time mix, hours worked, birth-death adjustment — will matter as much as the headline.

## This week ahead

## Fed watch

The target range holds at 3.50–3.75%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.63% and SOFR at 3.65% — both sitting comfortably inside the corridor. The 10Y–3M spread has turned decisively positive at +88 bps (up 1 bp yesterday), a meaningful shift from the prolonged inversion that preceded this easing cycle. Nothing in the `calendar_week_ahead` flags an FOMC statement, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release in the next five business days — so this morning's NFP print and next week's Consumer Credit data are the primary inputs into Fed signaling until the next scheduled communication. The oil spike is the wildcard: WTI north of $91 and Brent approaching $96 will force the Committee to square any further easing bias against renewed goods-price pressure.
