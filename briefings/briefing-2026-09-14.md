## Top of mind

The bond market is the story heading into a week dominated by Wednesday's FOMC decision. The 10-year closed Thursday at 4.95% — up 12 bps in a single session — and the 30-year is sitting at 5.37%, levels that will test the Fed's communication on the pace of any further easing. That rate backup landed alongside Friday's August CPI print and a preliminary Michigan sentiment read, and the 2s10s spread remains a modest +39 bps (4.56%/4.95%), so the curve is positively sloped but the long end is doing real damage to rate-sensitive sectors. Brent crude at $109.51 and WTI at $97.26 — both up roughly $3 on the week as Oman-Iran talks on Hormuz access collapsed — add a meaningful upside risk to the inflation path the FOMC will be projecting Wednesday.

## Yesterday's releases (Fri 9/11)

- **CPI (August):** Headline index at 334.131 vs. 332.813 in July (+1.318 index points, roughly +0.40% m/m); core at 337.765 vs. 336.789 (+0.29% m/m). That headline acceleration, driven in part by energy, is exactly what the oil price surge threatens to extend into September. The Fed gets this number 72 hours before its statement.
- **Consumer Sentiment — Preliminary (September):** Michigan sentiment data in the payload reflects the July final read (55.2 vs. 49.5 in June); the preliminary September release from Friday has not yet posted to FRED. Watch for the revision on 9/25. Inflation expectations in the July series were 4.2% vs. 4.6% prior — a modest improvement but still well above the Fed's target anchor.

## This week ahead

- **Tue 9/15:** Empire State Manufacturing (8:30, NY Fed) — first look at September manufacturing conditions; the August Philly Fed GAS index surged to 47.4 from 41.4, so regional manufacturing momentum is worth watching for confirmation.
- **Wed 9/16:** **FOMC Statement (14:00)** — the main event; also Retail Sales (8:30), Import/Export Prices (8:30), and NAHB Housing Market Index (10:00). Retail Sales for August will be the last major consumption read the Fed sees before the statement; July came in at $763.6B, down from $768.1B in June, so the consumption trajectory is already softening.
- **Thu 9/17:** Housing Starts (8:30), Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing (8:30), Initial Jobless Claims (8:30) — starts fell hard in July (1,239K from 1,415K) even as permits ticked up to 1,433K, so the August starts print will clarify whether that was noise or rate-driven pullback. Claims have been remarkably stable at 206–207K.
- **Fri 9/18:** Industrial Production (9:15, Fed) — July IP was 102.99 with capacity utilization at 76.3%; August should confirm whether the modest manufacturing pickup has legs.

## Fed watch

Target range is 3.50–3.75%; the effective fed funds rate is running at 3.63% and SOFR is 3.62%, both well-behaved within the corridor. The FOMC Statement drops Wednesday at 14:00 ET — this is a live meeting with a Summary of Economic Projections and press conference, so the updated dot plot and growth/inflation forecasts will be the key deliverables. The combination of a hot August CPI print, crude oil pushing toward $100 WTI on geopolitical supply risk, and a 10-year that has repriced 12 bps in a day gives the Committee cover to hold and signal a higher-for-longer posture, but the GDPNow tracker at 4.4% for Q3 complicates any dovish pivot — growth is not the problem right now.
