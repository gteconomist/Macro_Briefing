## Top of mind

The dominant macro story is the sharp oil price break: WTI fell from $86.16 to $81.96 (−$4.20) and Brent from $96.95 to $88.90 (−$8.05) in just the move from July 31 to August 3, a disinflationary impulse that reinforces the already-soft June CPI and PCE readings. Offsetting that, the Strait of Hormuz headline this morning — Trump flagging a possible deal with Iran via Oman talks — introduces two-way risk on energy prices that warrants close attention. Rates rallied modestly on the week (10Y down 7 bps to 4.63%, 2Y down 5 bps to 4.20%), and the Atlanta Fed GDPNow for Q3 sits at a striking 5.86%, up sharply from 1.54% in Q1, suggesting the demand side hasn't rolled over despite the inflation retreat.
