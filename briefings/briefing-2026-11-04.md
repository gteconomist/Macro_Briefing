## Top of mind

Democrats flipping the Senate majority is the dominant macro-relevant political event this morning — it reshapes the legislative calculus on fiscal policy, debt ceiling negotiations, and any prospective tax legislation heading into the next budget cycle. Against that backdrop, the Treasury curve has continued to sell off: the 10-year closed Tuesday at 4.95% (+4 bps), the 30-year at 5.32% (+4 bps), and the 2s10s spread widened to 34 bps. The T10Y3M spread is now +74 bps — no longer inverted — which removes a recession signal but also means the market is pricing meaningful term premium into long rates, likely a mix of fiscal risk and sticky inflation expectations.

## Yesterday's releases (Tue 11/3)

- **Factory Orders (Full Report):** No FRED value in payload for yesterday's release. Watch for the durable/non-durable split and core capex (nondefense capital goods ex-aircraft) orders revision as the signal on business investment momentum.

At 7.5M openings against roughly 7M unemployed, the jobs-to-workers ratio ticked back above 1.0, complicating any Fed narrative that labor market slack is building.

## Fed watch

Target range holds at 3.75–4.00%; the effective fed funds rate is running at 3.88% (as of 11/2) and SOFR at 3.95% (as of 11/2), both consistent with normal corridor mechanics. With the 10-year at 4.95% and 30-year at 5.32%, financial conditions are tightening via the long end independent of any FOMC action — a de facto tightening the Committee will note but cannot control. No FOMC meeting, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through the end of this week; Friday's employment report is the next major data input that could shift the rate path conversation heading into the December meeting.
