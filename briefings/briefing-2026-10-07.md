## Top of mind

The 10-year Treasury closed Tuesday at 5.27% (off 4 bps from Monday's 5.31%), but the level itself remains the dominant macro constraint — a 10Y3M spread of +106 bps confirms the curve has fully uninverted and is pricing in sustained higher-for-longer at the long end. WTI front-month settled at $89.44 and Brent at $100.58, essentially flat on the day but Brent's triple-digit handle adds another layer of cost pressure heading into September CPI (not yet released). The FOMC Minutes drop at 14:00 today and will be the day's focal point — markets will be parsing the internal debate around the pace of any additional easing given that the 2-year sits at 4.79%, 79 bps above the top of the target range.

## Yesterday's releases (Tue 10/6)

- **JOLTS (August):** Job openings came in at 7,079K (as of 2026-08-01), down 256K from July's 7,335K — the fourth consecutive month of decline and the weakest print since early 2024. The trend is unambiguous: labor demand is cooling, and the quits-sensitive components are worth watching when the full release hits. Consistent with the September unemployment rate nudging up to 4.2%.

## Today's calendar (Wed 10/7, ET)

- **FOMC Minutes, 14:00** — The September meeting cut or hold decision and the internal disagreement around it are the read. Watch for any shift in how members characterized the inflation-versus-labor-market tradeoff, and whether the committee discussed pace of balance sheet runoff or any threshold language around future moves.
- **Consumer Credit (G.19), 15:00** — The most recent FRED data (July, as of 2026-07-01) showed total consumer credit at $5.186T, up ~$18.1B from June, with revolving credit ($1.357T) up ~$2.8B and non-revolving ($3.829T) up ~$15.3B. Today's release covers August — the directional question is whether revolving credit growth is re-accelerating (a household stress signal at these interest rates) or flattening as consumers pull back.

## This week ahead

- **Thu 10/8:** Initial Jobless Claims (8:30) — last print was 197K for the week ending 9/26, the lowest since spring; any move back toward 210K+ would be notable given the JOLTS softening.
- **Fri 10/9:** Consumer Sentiment — Preliminary UMich (10:00) — the August final was 51.7, down sharply from 55.2 in July. Inflation expectations from this survey (August 1-year at 4.0%) matter to the Fed; a further deterioration in sentiment with sticky inflation expectations would complicate the easing path.

## Fed watch

The target range remains 3.75%–4.00% (DFEDTARU/DFEDTARL as of 10/6). The effective fed funds rate is printing at 3.88% (as of 10/5), and SOFR is at 3.89% — both hugging the midpoint of the range cleanly. The 2-year at 4.79% signals the market is not pricing aggressive near-term easing; the spread between the 2Y and the top of the target range (79 bps) is the widest it's been since early in the hiking cycle. Today's FOMC Minutes are the only Fed communication event this week — no speakers, no SEP, no Beige Book on the calendar through Friday — so the Minutes carry full interpretive weight for gauging committee cohesion on the pace of any further cuts.
