## Top of mind

The dominant macro story is the geopolitical squeeze on energy markets. Houthi strikes on Saudi oil facilities and Iran's stated intent to expand control over the Strait of Hormuz are pushing crude sharply higher — WTI at $91.48 (+$4.45 from the prior reading) and Brent at $96.02 (+$6.27), the latter now approaching triple digits. That oil spike lands directly in the August CPI print due Friday, complicating the Fed's read on whether the disinflation trend is intact. Canada's $27.6B in retaliatory tariffs taking effect adds a separate upstream price pressure channel, particularly for goods.

## Fed watch

The target range holds at 3.50–3.75%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.63% and SOFR at 3.66% — both sitting tightly within the corridor. Nothing in the calendar through Friday involves FOMC communications (no minutes, no Beige Book, no SEP). The policy tension is straightforward: the labor market is holding (U-3 at 4.1%, nonfarm payrolls +162K in August, LFPR ticking up to 61.6%), which removes urgency to cut, while the energy-driven inflation risk from the Hormuz/Houthi situation could push the August CPI print meaningfully above July's trend — exactly the wrong setup for a dovish pivot. Friday's number will carry unusual weight for September FOMC positioning.
