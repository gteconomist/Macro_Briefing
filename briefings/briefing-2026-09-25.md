## Top of mind

The dominant macro signal this morning is the sharp overnight jump in crude: WTI front-month settled at $94.61 (+$2.45) and Brent at $106.60 (+$3.52) on Thursday. That move is almost certainly geopolitical — Iran ceasefire negotiations, Netanyahu at the UN, and a Senate Iran war-powers vote are all in the headline mix — but the magnitude matters for the inflation path. The 10-year Treasury par yield closed Thursday at 5.18 (+7 bps), the 30-year at 5.47 (+7 bps), and the 3-month at 4.24 (+5 bps), so term rates are moving in the same direction as energy costs simultaneously. The T10Y3M spread is now +94 bps, uninverted and widening — a rate-path and term-premium story that bears watching alongside today's durable goods print.

## Fed watch

The target range remains 3.75–4.00%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.88% (as of 9/23) and SOFR at 3.87% — both running near the midpoint of the band, as expected. The 7-bp single-session backup in the 10- and 30-year par yields on Thursday is doing some of the Fed's work in tightening financial conditions, but with WTI now above $94 and Brent above $106, the energy channel is a complication for the last-mile inflation narrative. No FOMC statement, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release appears in the calendar through the end of next week, so the next formal policy signal will come from speeches and the October meeting cycle.
