## Top of mind

July retail sales came in soft — Census reported $763.6B, down $4.5B from June's $768.1B, with ex-motor vehicles also off ($622.2B vs. $624.1B) — the first month-over-month decline in the series in several months and a meaningful signal on consumer spending momentum heading into Q3. That said, the GDPNow tracker sits at 4.3% for Q3 (vs. 1.5% last quarter), regional Fed manufacturing surveys for July surged (NY Fed general activity +9.9 pts to 15.6; Philly +31.1 pts to 41.4), and the labor market printed an unemployment rate tick-down to 4.1% in July, so the softness in retail looks isolated rather than systemic for now. The key question this week is whether Wednesday's FOMC Minutes reveal any shift in the committee's reaction function given the divergence between strong activity surveys and weakening consumer spending.
