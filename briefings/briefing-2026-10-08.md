## Top of mind

The 10-year Treasury closed Wednesday at 5.28% — up 1 bp on the day and sitting at a level that keeps real financing costs firmly restrictive — while the 30-year pushed to 5.67%, a 3 bp move that matters for mortgage and long-duration corporate pricing. The curve is steeply positive (T10Y3M spread at +106 bps), signaling the market has largely repriced away any near-term recession risk, but long-end pressure is building. The FOMC Minutes released yesterday will be the interpretive lens this morning: with the effective funds rate at 3.88% against a 3.75–4.00% target band, the question is how much optionality the Committee is signaling before November. Brent settled just above $100/bbl; any sustained hold there feeds into the next CPI print and complicates the Fed's trajectory.

## Yesterday's releases (Wed 10/7)

- **FOMC Minutes (September meeting):** Released 14:00. The key signal to extract is the Committee's characterization of the easing pace and any dissent language around the degree of restrictiveness — with DFF at 3.88% and the 10-year at 5.28%, the long end is doing some work, but the minutes will clarify how much weight members are putting on that versus incoming labor and inflation data.

- **Consumer Credit (August):** Total consumer credit outstanding came in at $5,196,778M, up ~$8,281M from July's $5,188,498M. The composition is notable: revolving credit *fell* $4,787M month-over-month (from $1,357,201M to $1,352,414M), while non-revolving rose $13,067M (from $3,831,297M to $3,844,364M). The revolving pullback — credit cards and lines — is worth watching given still-elevated rates; it may reflect either consumer caution or charge-off-driven tightening on the lender side.
