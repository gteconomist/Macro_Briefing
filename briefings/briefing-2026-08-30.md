## Top of mind

The dominant macro story heading into the week is the Friday NFP print (9/4), which arrives against a backdrop of a labor market showing early softness: July payrolls came in at 158,858K — essentially flat from June's 158,881K — while the JOLTS openings reading for June slipped to 7,359K from 7,537K. Overnight, the geopolitical risk premium sharpened considerably: U.S. strikes on Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island directly threaten Strait of Hormuz transit, yet WTI and Brent were already down hard on the week — WTI at $83.90 (-$2.44 from the prior day), Brent at $88.24 (-$4.47) — meaning the market will need to re-price energy risk at Monday's open. Gold off ~$4.88 to $4,455 and silver down ~$4.49 to $66.37 suggest some risk-off unwind was already underway before the strikes became public.

## Yesterday's releases (Fri 8/28)

## This week ahead

- **Tue 9/1:** **ISM Manufacturing** (10:00) — the August read; NY Fed and Philly Fed general activity indices for August both improved (20.6 and 47.4, respectively), suggesting the manufacturing survey could show continued expansion, but watch the orders/prices-paid components given the Hormuz escalation. **JOLTS** (10:00) — July openings; June slipped to 7,359K and the trend matters for the Fed's labor-demand narrative ahead of Friday's employment report.
- **Wed 9/2:** **ADP National Employment** (8:15) — August private payrolls; the leading indicator for Friday's BLS print given flat July establishment survey. **Factory Orders — Full Report** (10:00) — completes the durable-goods picture.
- **Thu 9/3:** **Initial Jobless Claims** (8:30) — claims have been well-behaved at 203K (week of 8/22), down from 207K; a sustained sub-210K print keeps the labor market soft-landing story intact. **Productivity and Costs** (8:30) — Q2 final revision; unit labor costs are the key inflation input here. **ISM Services** (10:00) — with goods activity firming, the services read will determine whether the broad economy is accelerating into Q3.
- **Fri 9/4:** **Employment Report** (8:30) — the week's marquee release. July payrolls were essentially flat MoM; August needs to show whether that was noise or trend. Unemployment at 4.1% (down from 4.2%), participation at 61.4%, and AHE at $32.40 set the baseline. A second consecutive weak payroll print would substantially shift rate-cut timing expectations.

## Fed watch

The target range sits at 3.50–3.75%, with the effective fed funds rate at 3.63% and SOFR at 3.64% — both printing in the middle of the band and essentially unchanged week-over-week, indicating no technical pressure on overnight markets. The 2s/10s spread is 47 bps (4.20% vs. 4.67%) and the 10Y/3M spread is 83 bps — curve has been steepening, which historically signals that markets are pricing eventual easing but not urgently. Nothing in the week-ahead calendar flags an FOMC statement, minutes, Beige Book, or SEP release, so Fed communication this week will be entirely data-dependent — the ISM pair, claims, and especially Friday's employment report will drive market pricing for the September meeting. The Hormuz escalation adds an asymmetric upside risk to energy CPI that the Committee will need to weigh against what has been a modest improvement in core PCE (130.658 vs. 130.338 in June).
